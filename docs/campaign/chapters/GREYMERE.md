# The Hollow — Chapter 1: Greymere

This file records the revised Greymere Catalyst identity and the scene-level clues that support it. It is intended to preserve the existing Greymere flow, maps, encounters, and most dialogue while making the first Hollow feel more specific to the campaign's magic system.

## Chapter identity

Greymere remains the simple opening chapter. It should not compete with later locations in scale, but it should establish an important rule of the setting:

**A Hollow can corrupt or amplify something that already exists in a person or object until that thing becomes the local Catalyst.**

The Hollow itself is not treated as having a standalone combat technique. The strange combat identity belongs to the Catalyst the Hollow has twisted.

Do not imply that every Catalyst must begin as a person with an existing magical technique. Greymere is the party's first example of this kind of corruption, not a complete explanation of all Hollows.

## The Catalyst — Harlan Voss, The Warden

### Before the Hollow

**Harlan Voss** was once a real protector around Greymere. Hunters and travelers called him **the Warden**.

Voss possessed a small, unusual form of personal sorcery:

- he physically drew a closed circle around a campsite
- for a short time, ordinary beasts and physical threats resisted or failed to cross inward
- the effect was protective, limited, and practical rather than overwhelming
- hunters copied his circular marks as good-luck symbols even when they could not reproduce the magic

Voss eventually found the strange dead patch north of Greymere. Believing his own technique could contain it, he tried to place boundaries around the anomaly.

He disappeared.

Greymere gradually treated him as dead or lost.

### After corruption

The Hollow amplified Voss's existing boundary magic until his circles could partition space itself.

His original instinct was:

**keep danger out.**

The corrupted version became:

**keep everyone in.**

Voss became a Wight-like Catalyst and began abducting people, animals, and travelers. He does not think of his prisoners as victims. He believes he has created the only truly safe place and that anyone trying to leave is choosing danger.

This explains:

- the captured villagers
- Jess/Pipp's father being imprisoned rather than killed
- Zach/Echo's Dog being kept alive
- the jail-cell area
- the existing circular battlefield mechanic in the Catalyst room

## Boss technique — Closed Circle

Keep the current Wight statistics and the existing circular cut-off mechanic.

The circles are now the visual expression of Voss's amplified technique: **Closed Circle**.

When a circle activates:

- describe a thin pale boundary appearing across the floor
- when it closes, the ring becomes unnaturally absolute
- that section of the room is cut off according to the existing encounter mechanic
- Voss treats this as protection, not punishment

Useful boss lines:

- "Stay inside the line."
- "I can keep you safe."
- "Stop leaving."
- At low HP: "Why do you keep choosing the outside?"

Do not have Voss explain his own history during the fight. The players should piece the identity together through earlier clues and the prisoners.

## Existing scene flow

Preserve:

**Greymere → The Crooked Stag → North Woods → Edrin's Camp → Hollow Approach → Hollow Dungeon → Catalyst Room**

Do not rebuild the maps solely for this revision.

## Greymere clues

### Guards

One guard can tell the party that Harlan Voss once patrolled the north woods and was known for drawing protective circles around camps.

A second guard says Edrin recently mentioned finding a fresh-looking old Warden mark near his traps. The guard dismissed it because Voss has been gone for years.

The guards do not know that Voss is alive or that he became the Catalyst.

### Hobb — preserve exactly

Do **not** reinterpret or rewrite Hobb's line:

> "Whole world's got walls. You just can't see 'em from this side. EhHEHeHEHEe"

This is separate, campaign-scale foreshadowing about the world itself and should continue to sound like nonsense from an insane person. It is not a clue about Voss's circle technique.

## The Crooked Stag

### Jimmy Frog

Preserve his normal shop/barkeep role.

He can clarify that the `VII` carving behind the bar is **not** one of Voss's old marks. Voss's marks were circles.

This is important because it keeps `VII` as its own mystery instead of accidentally explaining it through the Warden story.

### Suspicious Man

Keep his existing explanation of Hollows and Catalysts, with one additional useful idea:

A Catalyst can be something the Hollow **twisted** rather than something it simply created from nothing.

He may also warn the party not to assume that circles found near the Hollow are protective.

## North Woods

Keep:

- the hidden `VII` tree carving
- the Dire Wolf encounter
- Dog's tracks

Optional clue if the party searches the area beyond the `VII` tree:

- **DC 13 Investigation:** find an old, faded charcoal circle around the remains of a campsite
- it is harmless and clearly much older than the recent disappearances
- this is an example of the mundane version of Voss's old warding practice

Do not connect the `VII` carving to Voss.

## Edrin's Camp

Keep all established clues and DCs.

### Edrin's Knife

The existing Knife discoverable is now explicitly **Edrin's Knife**.

When found:

- the initials `E.V.` remain
- **DC 12 Investigation:** notice a small hand-scratched circle on the inside of the sheath
- Edrin copied the circular mark after finding a fresh-looking one near his traps

Additional environmental clue:

- a partial ring is scored into the dirt near the edge of camp
- the ring is broken outward
- the existing drag marks continue from that area toward the Hollow

This should suggest that something using a protective-looking boundary was involved without revealing Voss yet.

## Hollow Approach

Keep the established greying/dead-forest visuals.

Add one automatic environmental detail:

An old ring of stones sits beside the path. The stones outside the ring are undisturbed; several stones on the inside have been shoved outward.

No check is required to notice it if the party is paying attention to the path.

## Hollow Dungeon

### Room 1

Keep the Zombie encounter.

Optional visual clue:

One corpse has a faint pale circular mark around a wrist, as if a boundary had once been drawn directly around the limb.

Do not explain it.

### Puzzle room

Keep the existing **Tongue** riddle unchanged.

### Circular hazard / Catalyst foreshadowing

Any existing circular battlefield hazards should visually resemble simplified or imperfect versions of Voss's later Closed Circle technique. This creates recognition without requiring a new subsystem.

## Catalyst Room

The Wight instance is player-facing as **The Warden**.

Use the existing Wight statistics and circular room mechanic.

Voss should be calm and possessive rather than theatrical or sadistic.

He believes:

- the Hollow beyond his controlled space is dangerous
- people inside his lines are protected
- leaving is irrational and self-destructive
- force is justified if it keeps someone "safe"

## Prisoner dialogue / reveal

After or during the rescue, prisoners can establish the truth in pieces.

### Mr. Karew

He explains that the Warden repeatedly said the prisoners were safe and created new circles whenever someone tried to escape.

### Edrin Vale

Edrin is found alive in the jail-cell area. This is the direct payoff to the guards asking the party to find him and to the trail from Edrin's Camp.

He can confirm that he found what looked like a fresh Warden circle near his traps. After investigating it, he became confused and remembers being dragged toward the Hollow. If the party mentions his knife, he recognizes it immediately.

### Rescued guard

The guard recognizes the ruined face as Harlan Voss from old stories and images.

### Rescued villager

The villager remembers Voss insisting that he had finally created somewhere nothing could reach them, immediately before locking them in.

Together, these clues allow the party to understand Voss without a single exposition dump.

## Player-facing revelation after Greymere

By the end of the chapter, observant players can reasonably conclude:

- Harlan Voss had unusual boundary magic before he disappeared
- the being in the Hollow was Harlan Voss
- his harmless/protective technique became dramatically stronger and more dangerous
- his protective instinct was corrupted along with the technique
- the Hollow can therefore distort and amplify something that already exists in a Catalyst

They should **not** yet know:

- whether every Catalyst is created this way
- what `VII` means
- the full origin of Hollows
- the final world-scale truth

## DM tone

Greymere should still feel like the smaller opening chapter. Its job is to establish the campaign's language:

- strange people can possess highly specific forms of magic
- those powers have understandable identities and rules
- Hollows can corrupt those powers into something much larger
- a boss mechanic should feel like an extension of who the Catalyst was, not an arbitrary dungeon effect

Later chapters can become much stranger and more ambitious.
