# Veyrholt — Answering Bell Mechanic Revision

## Status

This document is the current canon for Veyrholt's bell mechanic.

It **supersedes every older Veyrholt rule that says attacks, spells, movement, or other physical actions are recorded and replayed six seconds later**. The chapter story, locations, NPC motives, missing-tithe mystery, Bell Regent identity, constructed-Catalyst revelation, and Lornwatch hook remain intact unless this document explicitly changes them.

The old term **Second Motion** may still appear in older notes. Mechanically, replace it with **the Answer** as defined here.

## Design goal

Veyrholt should still feel built around bells and a six-second supernatural rhythm, but the DM should not have to remember or recreate everything every creature did one round ago.

The mechanic should become something the players understand and learn to control over the chapter. By the Bell Regent fight, the party should recognize the rule immediately and use it against the Catalyst on purpose.

The intended player realization is:

**"When a bell rings, we have six seconds. If we ring another tuned bell before the Hollow answers, we can decide where the answer goes."**

## Core rule — Call and Answer

Only specially tuned Veyrholt bells interact with the Hollow this way. An ordinary bell is just a bell.

1. A tuned bell rings. This creates a **Call**.
2. About six seconds later, the Hollow produces an **Answer**.
3. If no other tuned bell rings during that window, the Answer resolves through the original bell or its intended target.
4. If another tuned bell rings before the six seconds are up, the **last tuned bell rung becomes the destination of the Answer**.
5. A later ring can therefore steal, ground, or redirect a pending Answer.

The Answer is raw Hollow resonance shaped by the bell network. It does **not** copy the attack, spell, movement, healing, or other action that happened previously.

## Combat timing

Use one simple timing point instead of initiative 20 / initiative 10 bookkeeping.

- **Initiative 20:** resolve the previous Answer, then start the next Call if the encounter uses one.
- The party then has the entire round to react.
- Ringing a prepared encounter bell while adjacent is a **bonus action** unless the scene says otherwise.
- The DM should visibly mark the pending danger and clearly identify the most recently rung tuned bell.
- At the next initiative 20, resolve the Answer at that destination.

Do not hide the timing once the party has learned it. The challenge is positioning and choosing where to send the power, not guessing an invisible timer.

## How the party learns it

### 1. Bellpost Road — learn that an Answer can be grounded

The unstable road produces a Call every round during the Greyhook encounter.

The recovered seven-notch handbell is the safe ground point. If a character rings it before the next Answer, the Hollow's force goes into the handbell instead of bursting across the road.

This should be obvious after the first demonstration. The goal is to teach:

**Bell rings -> six seconds -> something answers. A prepared bell can catch it.**

### 2. Nessa Calder — explain the rule clearly

Nessa gives the party the plain-language explanation:

- the old bells are tuned to the Hollow
- a ring starts a six-second Call
- the Hollow answers after that delay
- the last tuned bell rung before the Answer controls where the force goes
- the old builders used this to make the Hollow predictable

Nessa should not speak in mystical riddles when explaining this. She is the person who makes the mechanic understandable.

### 3. Old Tithe Barn — learn to redirect instead of merely ground

The inherited livestock bells are receiving a Call from Castle Veyr and pulling the animals back onto the ancient tithe route.

During the Bailiff encounter, ringing the barn's tuned bell before the next Answer redirects the pull away from the animals and into the barn bell. The redirected force can knock nearby Bailiffs prone.

The lesson becomes:

**We are not just blocking this. We can move it.**

### 4. Castle Court — use redirection as access/control

The processional defenses use the same bell network.

A pending castle Answer marks a patrol/processional lane. Ringing the gate bell during the six-second window redirects the Answer into the gate plinth and briefly disables the Processional Echo.

This proves the party can use the castle's own system against its defenses.

### 5. Bellfoundry — turn the Answer into a weapon

The foundry sends a pending Answer into a marked pour channel.

If the party rings the true foundry bell or uses the tuned key before the Answer resolves, they can send the resonance into a prepared mold instead. The overloaded mold cracks violently and can damage nearby enemies.

The lesson becomes:

**The Answer has enough force to break the things that were built to carry it.**

That is the final mechanical clue for the Bell Regent.

## Bell Regent Catalyst fight

The Bell Regent is sustained by three tuned anchor bells in the belfry.

### The three anchors

1. **Ward Anchor**
   - While intact, the Bell Regent keeps its resistance to nonmagical bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage.
   - Destroying it removes that resistance.

2. **Command Anchor**
   - While intact, the Bell Regent can use **Commanding Toll** to pull a creature toward an anchor on a failed save.
   - Destroying it removes Commanding Toll.

3. **Crown Anchor**
   - While intact, the Bell Regent has AC 16.
   - Destroying it lowers the Bell Regent to AC 14.

Each anchor is AC 13 with 14 HP, immune to psychic and poison damage, and resistant to ordinary weapon damage. Thunder damage bypasses that resistance.

### The Regent's Call

At initiative 20:

1. Resolve the previous Call.
2. The Bell Regent starts a new Call.
3. Mark one visible **15-foot-radius danger zone** in the belfry.

If no anchor bell is rung before the next initiative 20, the Answer detonates in the marked zone:

- DC 13 Dexterity save
- 2d6 force damage on a failure
- pushed 10 feet on a failure
- half damage and no push on a success

### Stealing the Answer

Any creature adjacent to an intact anchor bell can ring it as a bonus action.

If one or more anchors are rung during the six-second window, the **last anchor rung** receives the Answer instead of the marked danger zone.

A redirected Answer:

- cancels the marked danger-zone effect
- deals **14 thunder damage** to that anchor
- ignores the anchor's resistance

The party can also attack anchors normally. They do not have to solve the fight only one way.

### Why this is the payoff

The party has already seen every part of the solution:

- Bellpost: an Answer can be caught.
- Barn: an Answer can be redirected.
- Court: the castle obeys the same rule.
- Foundry: redirected resonance can break the system carrying it.
- Belfry: redirect the Bell Regent's own power into its anchors.

The players should feel like they learned a Hollow rule and are now exploiting it, not like the boss introduced a brand-new puzzle in the final room.

## Bell Regent normal actions

The Bell Regent still fights normally between Calls.

- **Multiattack:** two Bellstaff attacks.
- **Bellstaff:** +5 to hit, 1d8+3 bludgeoning.
- **Commanding Toll (Recharge 5–6):** one creature within 60 feet makes a DC 13 Wisdom save. On a failure, it moves up to 15 feet toward an intact anchor by the safest available path. This action is lost when the Command Anchor is destroyed.
- **Toll of Assignment (once at half HP):** each creature makes a DC 13 Wisdom save. On a failure, its speed becomes 0 until it uses an action to reject the assigned position. Pipp's tuning key or speaking Caldris's name grants advantage.

## Lore preserved by the new mechanic

The founders did not create the underlying Hollow.

They discovered that tuned bronze could **steer its delayed response**, then built a civic containment network around that behavior. Bells at roads, farms, gates, workshops, and Castle Veyr became parts of the same system. Later generations remembered the customs but forgot the engineering.

Ser Caldris Veyr eventually became the Bell Regent, a constructed Catalyst and living part of that containment system.

This still establishes the chapter's intended revelation:

**A Catalyst can be deliberately built as a restraint around something older.**

It does not reveal the origin of all Hollows, the campaign-scale truth about Elyndra, the meaning of VII, or why Greymere and Veyrholt changed on the same night.

## Dialogue rule for Veyrholt

Veyrholt NPCs should speak in clear modern conversational English unless a specific character trait requires otherwise.

Avoid phrases that sound deliberately archaic, ceremonial, or cryptic when the NPC is conveying information the players need to understand.

Especially when explaining Hollow lore or mechanics:

- say what happened directly
- use short concrete sentences
- distinguish what the NPC knows from what they are guessing
- reserve poetic or old-fashioned language for inscriptions, ancient oaths, historical text, or deliberate atmosphere

The players should never miss important lore because the wording sounded like a riddle.
