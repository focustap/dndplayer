# Veyrholt — Bell & Second Motion Revision

## Status

This document is the current canon for Veyrholt's bell mechanic.

It supersedes the previous `Call and Answer` revision and any older rule that required the DM to replay every attack, spell, or movement six seconds later.

The chapter story, locations, NPC motives, missing-tithe mystery, Bell Regent identity, constructed-Catalyst revelation, and Lornwatch hook remain intact unless this document explicitly changes them.

## Core idea

Veyrholt's people have an old habit:

**When the first bell rings, stop. When the second bell rings, continue.**

Most people alive today do not know why. Their parents taught them. Their parents learned it from their parents. It has become ordinary Veyrholt etiquette and superstition.

The forgotten reason is that the six seconds between those bells are genuinely dangerous.

The bells do not cause the Hollow effect. Veyrholt's ancestors learned the timing of a recurring unstable interval inside the Hollow and built the bell network to warn people exactly when that interval began and ended.

The first bell means: **do not make an important move right now.**

The second bell means: **the danger has passed.**

## The Second Motion

When the first bell rings, the Hollow begins listening for a **First Motion**.

The first meaningful physical change made before the second bell is remembered by the Hollow.

About six seconds later, when the second bell rings, the Hollow produces a distorted **Second Motion** based on that first change.

Only **one** First Motion is stored per bell cycle. Once something has been caught, later actions in that same six-second window are not stored by that cycle.

If nobody does anything meaningful between the bells, nothing is repeated.

This is why the safest old rule was simply to freeze until the second bell.

### What counts as a meaningful motion

Use common sense. Examples include:

- moving more than a few feet
- making a weapon attack
- casting a spell with an obvious physical effect
- opening, closing, pulling, striking, dropping, or moving a substantial object
- ringing or striking a bell

Small speech, breathing, looking around, or trivial hand movements do not matter.

## Important simplification

The DM does **not** replay the original action exactly.

The Second Motion repeats the **physical change or force** of the First Motion in a simple encounter-specific way.

Examples:

- **Movement:** a force-trace follows the same route and can shove or damage creatures standing in it.
- **Weapon attack or spell impact:** the original impact point produces a short force burst instead of rolling the attack or spell again.
- **Door, lever, gate, or machinery:** the object tries to repeat the same movement.
- **Striking a tuned bell:** the bell repeats its own vibration from inside the bronze, which can overload or crack it.

The table only tracks **one stored motion per bell cycle**.

## Why the city still stops

The exact technical rule was lost generations ago.

Modern Veyrholt residents generally do not know that only the first meaningful motion is caught. They just know the inherited rule:

> First bell: stop. Second bell: go.

People pause mid-conversation, merchants stop counting coins, carts stop at marked stones, workers put tools down, and children are taught not to run between the two tolls.

Most residents treat this like an old safety custom. Some think it is superstition. Very few understand that it began as practical Hollow engineering.

That cultural habit should be visible before anyone explains the real reason.

## Combat timing

For encounters that actively use the mechanic:

1. **First Toll:** announce the first bell and clear the previous Stored Motion.
2. The first meaningful action or movement after the toll becomes the **Stored Motion**.
3. Once a motion is stored, the rest of the round proceeds normally.
4. **Second Toll:** about six seconds later, resolve the scene's simple Second Motion effect.
5. The dangerous interval ends.

Do not make players guess whether a motion has been stored. Once they understand the rule, tell them clearly what the Hollow caught.

The interesting decision is **what should become the First Motion**, not remembering every action from the last round.

## How the party learns it

### 1. Bellpost Road — see why everyone freezes

The party hears the first toll.

A frightened traveler or Greyhook moves before the second toll.

When the second bell rings, a visible force-trace repeats that route and slams through the road.

This gives the party the first clue:

**Something done between the bells comes back.**

During the encounter, only the first meaningful motion each cycle is stored.

- stored movement -> force line along that route
- stored attack/spell impact -> small force burst at the impact point
- ringing the restored handbell as the First Motion -> the bell produces a controlled second resonance and disrupts the Road Refrains

This is the first moment where the party can intentionally choose a useful First Motion.

### 2. Veyrholt itself — show the tradition before explaining it

When the town bell rings, daily life stops almost instantly.

Nobody makes a big deal about it.

Six seconds later, the second bell rings and everyone continues what they were doing.

If the party asks why, ordinary residents give answers like:

- "That's just what you do here."
- "My mother would have killed me if I moved between bells."
- "Bad luck. Always has been."
- "First bell means wait. Second means go."

They should discover the habit before receiving the technical explanation.

### 3. Nessa Calder — explain the forgotten rule clearly

Nessa understands enough of the old bellwork to explain it plainly:

- the bells were built as warnings, not as the source of the effect
- the first toll marks a dangerous six-second interval
- the Hollow catches the first important motion made during that interval
- the second toll releases a distorted repeat of that motion
- the old builders learned that striking properly tuned bronze during the interval creates an unusually predictable Second Motion

Her key explanation is:

**"That's why everyone stops. The old rule wasn't manners. It was survival."**

### 4. Old Tithe Barn — learn that objects can be used on purpose

The ancient tithe system used timed gates, pens, bells, and marked routes.

The party can intentionally make a gate, bell, or other prepared mechanism become the First Motion. At the second toll, that mechanism's repeated movement can block a route, knock down a Bailiff, or free trapped livestock.

The lesson is:

**The Second Motion is dangerous, but it can be planned.**

### 5. Castle Court — learn to control the first motion under pressure

The processional defenses are built around the same timing rule.

If the wrong creature moves first after the toll, the castle turns that movement into a dangerous force path.

If the party deliberately uses a gate mechanism or other prepared object first, they can make the castle repeat something useful instead.

The lesson becomes:

**Do not just survive the six seconds. Decide what the Hollow remembers.**

### 6. Bellfoundry — discover the boss solution

The foundry reveals that tuned bronze handles Second Motions differently from ordinary objects.

If a bell or heavy tuned bronze mold is struck as the First Motion, the second toll reproduces the vibration **inside the metal itself**.

That internal second resonance can crack a mold, split old bronze, or damage creatures touching it.

This is the final clue before the Bell Regent:

**A tuned anchor can be broken by making its own vibration become the Second Motion.**

## Bell Regent Catalyst fight

The Bell Regent is sustained by three tuned anchor bells in the belfry.

### The anchors

1. **Ward Anchor**
   - While intact, the Bell Regent has resistance to nonmagical bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage.
   - Destroying it removes that resistance.

2. **Command Anchor**
   - While intact, the Bell Regent can use **Commanding Toll**.
   - Destroying it removes Commanding Toll.

3. **Crown Anchor**
   - While intact, the Bell Regent has AC 16.
   - Destroying it lowers the Bell Regent to AC 14.

Each anchor is AC 13 with 14 HP, immune to psychic and poison damage, and resistant to ordinary weapon damage. Thunder damage bypasses that resistance.

### Belfry bell cycle

At the First Toll, announce that the six-second interval has begun.

The **first meaningful motion** made in the belfry becomes the Stored Motion.

The party's ideal play is to already be positioned beside the anchor they want to break, then make their first meaningful action after the toll a deliberate strike or ring against that anchor.

Once that motion is stored, everyone can move and fight normally for the rest of the cycle.

At the Second Toll:

- if an anchor strike/ring was the Stored Motion, that anchor produces a violent internal Second Motion
- the anchor takes **14 thunder damage**, ignoring resistance
- this is not another attack roll and does not repeat the player's weapon attack
- it is the bell's own vibration being reproduced inside the bronze

The party can still attack the anchors normally. The timing mechanic is an advantage they learned, not the only legal solution.

### If the wrong motion is stored

If someone moves, attacks, or casts before the intended anchor motion is stored, the Hollow catches that instead.

Resolve one simple effect at the Second Toll:

- **stored movement:** a visible force line follows the movement path; creatures in it make DC 13 Dex saves or take 1d8 force and are pushed 5 feet
- **stored attack/spell impact:** a 5-foot burst appears at the original impact point; DC 13 Dex, 1d8 force on a failure
- **stored object motion:** the object repeats that movement if physically possible

Then the next bell cycle begins fresh.

This makes coordination matter without forcing the DM to replay an entire round.

### Bell Regent behavior

The Regent understands the old timing and does not mindlessly break the rule.

It tries to stop the party from controlling the First Motion by:

- pushing them away from anchors before a toll
- using **Commanding Toll** to pull creatures out of position
- attacking characters who are waiting beside an anchor
- using its own first attack as the Stored Motion if the party fails to claim the interval before its turn

This makes positioning before the First Toll important.

## Bell Regent normal actions

- **Multiattack:** two Bellstaff attacks.
- **Bellstaff:** +5 to hit, 1d8+3 bludgeoning.
- **Commanding Toll (Recharge 5-6):** one creature within 60 feet makes a DC 13 Wisdom save. On a failure, it moves up to 15 feet toward an intact anchor by the safest available path. This action is lost when the Command Anchor is destroyed.
- **Toll of Assignment (once at half HP):** each creature makes a DC 13 Wisdom save. On a failure, its speed becomes 0 until it uses an action to reject the assigned position. Pipp's tuning key or speaking Caldris's name grants advantage.

## Lore preserved by the new mechanic

The founders did not create the underlying Hollow.

They discovered a repeating six-second instability and learned how to predict it. The bell network was built to warn people when the Hollow was about to catch a motion and when the danger had passed.

Later builders discovered that tuned bronze produced especially stable, predictable Second Motions, which let them turn the bell network into part of the containment system around Castle Veyr.

Over generations, the engineering became tradition:

**first bell -> stop**

**second bell -> continue**

The people remembered the behavior and forgot the reason.

Ser Caldris Veyr eventually became the Bell Regent, a constructed Catalyst and living part of that containment system.

This still establishes the chapter's intended revelation:

**A Catalyst can be deliberately built as a restraint around something older.**

It does not reveal the origin of all Hollows, the campaign-scale truth about Elyndra, the meaning of VII, or why Greymere and Veyrholt changed on the same night.

## Dialogue rule for Veyrholt

Veyrholt NPCs should speak in clear modern conversational English unless a specific character trait requires otherwise.

Avoid archaic, ceremonial, or cryptic wording when an NPC is conveying information the players need to understand.

Especially when explaining Hollow lore or mechanics:

- say what happened directly
- use short concrete sentences
- distinguish what the NPC knows from what they are guessing
- reserve poetic or old-fashioned language for inscriptions, preserved oaths, historical text, or deliberate atmosphere

The players should never miss important lore because the wording sounded like a riddle.
