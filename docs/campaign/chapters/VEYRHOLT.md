## City history and the Dealer

Veyrholt is a large, old walled river city with docks, bridges, trade roads, churches, civic halls, poor waterside streets, noble gardens, shops and dense lived-in neighborhoods. It was not originally a gambling city. Most residents still work ordinary jobs and have never wagered anything.

A stranger arrived with almost nothing except beautifully illustrated cards. He accepted almost any game or challenge, matched every opponent's stake and never lost a public challenge. Nobody understood how he produced equal stakes. Coins became jewelry, weapons, enchanted objects, businesses, houses, land and noble possessions. Lady Ilyra Veyr, lawful owner of the central estate, eventually wagered House Veyr to recover earlier losses. Witnessed deeds establish her authority over that estate, not the city or its people. The Dealer won; no army conquered Veyrholt.

House Veyr was a noble estate/civic palace. The Dealer opened its grounds to challengers and built an enormous red/black/gold carnival pavilion, gaming halls, queues, booths and wagons around its old rooms. Performers, bookmakers, mercenaries, nobles, strange sorcerers and desperate former owners converged. This distorted the city's social center, not every district. The ordinary city remains visually and socially distinct.

**DM truth:** The oldest foundations enclosed a dormant Hollow. The Dealer did not know what he acquired. His original sorcery already governed declared games, acknowledged stakes, illustrated cards, winners and losers. Matched stakes originally appeared as escrow only during accepted games: unspendable elsewhere until won, never an unlimited cash source. His undefeated reputation is history, not combat immunity. The compatible Hollow amplified that technique into supernatural collection and ownership enforcement, corrupting him into its Catalyst. Enforcement affects the estate and voluntarily pledged objects, not arbitrary citizens or unagreed stakes.

He was charming, strange, confident and entertaining. Now he never sleeps, cards change illustrations, pledged objects drag themselves toward collection desks, and doors recognize claimants. He insists every dispute is solvable by rules and mistakes winning for moral authority. Play him as increasingly detached, occasionally kind and funny, never a cackling casino caricature.

## Investigation counterplay

Party resources for this encounter only; duplicate clues do not multiply charges. Keep in DM notes, explain learned rules to players. At most TWO interventions per round and ONE per creature's draw. Resolve in initiative order; the Dealer cannot veto or rewrite them.

- **Read the Back, 2 charges:** Before dealing, peek at next two cards and return in either order. No action. Arcana shop or training deck. Counts toward round cap.
- **Objection, 2 charges:** After selection, before turns, replace one kept card (including Dealer's) with next card, which must be kept. No action. Pell or Gallery.
- **Cut the Deck, 1 charge:** Phase two: after a pair is exposed, holder chooses instead. Targeting Dealer lets party choose his card. No action. Private House.
- **Burn a Card, 1 charge:** Between expiration and new draws, remove one named card from draw/discard permanently this fight. No action. Foundations. Counts toward cap.
- **Fallback:** Visible Final Table placard grants one party Objection charge if none learned. One character can spend an action at the table to earn one more Objection, once per encounter total, no roll, even if all research charges were spent.

## Final Table — runnable boss rules

Huge circular chamber, single central table, Pot behind warded glass/cages and elevated galleries, abandoned spectator balconies. No throne. Table/pillars give half cover; entry stair remains open; no arena damage pulses. Dealer sits shuffling and invites the party to sit. Talking, standing or refusing a wager are allowed. Confronting coercion, attacking, or agreeing a challenge starts initiative: **“Every game has rules.”** Combat is not consent to lose property. An agreed formal stake can match the party's challenge service against release of disputed claims; refusing a wager does not prevent victory breaking corruption.

### Profile

Medium humanoid; AC 14; HP 85; speed 30; STR 10 DEX 16 CON 14 INT 15 WIS 12 CHA 17; initiative +3; Dex save +5, Wis +3. No resistance, legendary resistance, sword or minions.

- Action **Card Volley:** two ranged spell attacks +5, 60 ft, each 1d6+3 force. Normal disadvantage with adjacent enemies. Spread attacks between conscious threats.
- Bonus action **Dealer's Step:** move 10 ft without opportunity attacks. Actual movement, so Chains prevents it; not teleportation.
- Reaction **Fold:** reduce incoming damage by 1d4 once/round after damage roll. Does not reduce printed HP costs.
- Saint grants Dealer and PCs the same healing action described below; Dealer has no healing action otherwise.
- Easier: 68 HP, one attack round one. Five rested PCs: 105 HP, same attacks. Three PCs: 60 HP, one attack/round. Do not change HP secretly mid-fight. Offer rest and emergency potions before entry if depleted.

### Round procedure and twelve-card deck

Before highest initiative EVERY round: expire cards and card temporary HP, resolve any returns, then draw for EVERY combatant including Dealer, participating pets, allies and summons. Keep nonparticipating Dog safely outside. New entrants draw immediately and again next round. Cards bind off-turn actions/reactions too and never wake unconscious creatures or grant extra turns.

Shuffle one set of twelve openly. Deal in initiative order without replacement. Expired/rejected cards go to discard. On exhaustion shuffle discard, never held/burned cards; if every available card is held use neutral blank until a card returns. Phase one is genuinely shuffled: no secret replacements. Reveal every kept card and demonstrate the Dealer's bad draws honestly; do not rig them. Public Chains demonstration already proves he is constrained.

| Card | Rule until next round |
| --- | --- |
| Blade | First melee hit on your turn adds 1d6 original-type damage. Cannot make ranged attacks; save spells and utility still work. |
| Glass | Once on your turn, add 1d6 to one damage roll. First damage you take this round adds 1d6 of that damage type. No bonus from reflected damage. |
| Saint | Cannot deal damage, including ongoing effects you control. Healing dice maximized. May use action to touch a creature (including self) restoring 8 HP, once per holder/round. Help, Dodge and nondamaging control allowed. |
| Dead Man | Optional: disappear until START of your turn this round, then return to same/nearest safe free space and take full turn. While absent cannot act, affect play or be targeted; concentration persists, controlled ongoing effects pause. May decline. |
| Crown | Advantage on your attacks; attacks against you have advantage. Normal cancellation applies. |
| Chains | Speed 0; immune to forced movement and being knocked prone. Teleportation allowed. If already prone, cannot stand until expiration. Actions remain available. |
| Mirror | First OTHER creature damaging you takes 1d6 force afterward, no reaction. Cannot trigger Mirror, Glass, Debt or any other card; self-cost does not trigger it. |
| Miser | Cannot regain HP. First time on your turn you damage another creature, gain 4 temporary HP; do not stack, leftovers expire next round. |
| Fool | First natural 1 on an attack becomes 20; first natural 20 becomes 1, each once, only the die used after advantage/disadvantage. Never convert the result again. No saves or death saves affected. |
| Duelist | Exactly one enemy within 5 ft at attack time: first hit on your turn adds 1d6. Two or more: attacks have disadvantage. None: no effect. |
| Hound | Move in straight segments of at least 10 ft before turning; may stop early. After 10 ft voluntary movement on your turn, next hit that turn adds 1d6. No forced movement/teleport credit. |
| Debt | First damage to another creature on your turn adds 1d6 to one roll, then lose 2 HP directly, minimum 1 HP. Not damage, ignores temp HP, cannot be reduced or trigger effects. |

Chapter rule: bonus card dice are not doubled by criticals. Only one held card, no stacking. No card removes a full player's turn. Normal spell/resource costs remain. Saint can suppress Mirror because its holder cannot deal damage. Rules apply equally to everyone.

### Phase two and tactics

At 42 HP or less, once: **“You didn't actually believe the cards were random.”** Announce now; current cards last through current round. Starting NEXT round everyone draws TWO; Dealer publicly chooses the kept card of each creature, himself included; discard other. No control of shuffle or card text. Show each pair and allow interventions before turns.

Selection policy: disrupt observed plan (Blade for archer, Chains for pursuer, Saint for striker); choose survival/ranged offense for himself. No hidden knowledge or invented harmful choice when both cards are useful. Choose promptly. Circle table, use cover, honor movement restrictions, heal/negotiate on Saint rounds, never execute fallen PCs. Retreat up foundations stairs ends pursuit.

Retreat permits recovery. Dealer recovers after long rest. Spent research resources can be replenished with another ten-minute source visit, each source only once more per chapter. Loss costs only explicitly agreed stakes or a civic complication, never automatic enslavement. Placard's mercy clause permits survivors to carry fallen allies upstairs.
