# The Hollow — Campaign Bible

This file contains established canon for Ben's D&D campaign **The Hollow**. Do not contradict it casually. Unknown details should remain open rather than being filled with arbitrary lore unless the current task specifically calls for invention.

## Core premise

The campaign takes place in the world of **Elyndra**.

Across Elyndra are forbidden regions known as **Hollows**: unnatural dead zones where reality, memory, life, and magic can behave incorrectly. Hollows are dangerous, poorly understood, and tied to ancient history.

Each Hollow has a **Catalyst** at its center. A Catalyst may be a creature, artifact, structure, ritual source, or another concrete cause sustaining the local Hollow. Destroying or neutralizing the Catalyst ends or collapses that Hollow.

### DM_SECRET — campaign-scale truth

The players are **not** supposed to learn early that the world itself is connected to a much larger Hollow truth. The campaign should reveal the nature and history of Hollows gradually across multiple locations.

Do not explain the complete cosmology, origin, or final truth of the Hollows in early chapters. Build toward it through partial discoveries, contradictions, symbols, historical fragments, and recurring phenomena.

## Tone and structure

The Hollow is mystery-driven dark fantasy rather than nonstop horror.

Important design traits:

- places initially feel like believable towns, roads, forests, ruins, camps, and communities
- strange details accumulate before the explanation does
- side plots can turn out to be connected to the main mystery
- recurring symbols and clues matter
- the players should feel they are learning rules about Hollows over time
- each Hollow should have its own identity or behavior instead of feeling like the same dungeon reskinned
- defeating a Catalyst should answer something while creating a new question

## Recurring mystery elements

### The mark: VII

The symbol/mark **VII** has appeared repeatedly around the Greymere Hollow.

Known uses so far include:

- carved into a tree in the North Woods
- carved into the Crooked Stag tavern wall
- recurring as an unexplained clue connected to the Hollow mystery

Its full meaning is not established for the players and should not be casually revealed.

### Memory / confusion

The Greymere material establishes themes of:

- disappearances
- confusion or panic near the Hollow
- memory-related mystery
- shared dreams / memory theft as campaign themes
- people being drawn or taken toward unnatural places

Future chapters may expand these ideas but should not make every Hollow function identically.

## Factions

At least two groups/factions in Elyndra are in recurring conflict.

## Chapter 1 — Greymere

### Greymere

Greymere is a small village near a strange forest and the first major campaign location.

The village has only a few dozen buildings around a worn stone square with a central well.

The area north of Greymere has become dangerous because of the spreading Hollow and recent disappearances.

### Guards

Three guards are normally visible with another patrolling.

They know:

- travelers should avoid the north after dark
- people have been disappearing
- the missing hunter **Edrin Vale** should be found
- Greymere has recently seen an unusual number of strangers

### The Crooked Stag

Greymere's tavern/inn is **The Crooked Stag**.

The barkeep is a frog named **Jimmy Frog**.

Important details:

- VII is carved into the wooden wall behind the bar
- if asked, Jimmy Frog says the mark was already there when he bought the tavern
- a suspicious man can point the party toward the north woods and the missing-hunter mystery
- the inn has a piano
- a Hearthstone item has been available for 5 gp; it teleports the party back to an inn

Do not revive the discarded barkeep name Bram Tallow unless the user explicitly changes canon.

### Wanted poster

Greymere has a wanted poster for a gnoll in the forest with a **50 gp reward**.

This can remain a side element unless deliberately tied into a later plot.

## Greymere northward sequence

The planned/established sequence is:

**Greymere → The Crooked Stag → North Woods → Edrin's Camp → Hollow Approach → Hollow Dungeon**

### North Woods

A hidden discoverable is a tree trunk carved with **VII**.

Interacting with/noticing it triggers an ambush by **3 Dire Wolves**.

An earlier lightweight combat profile used:

- attack: `d20 + 5`
- damage: `1d4 + 2`

Do not assume those numbers are mandatory for all future wolf uses.

### Edrin's Camp

The camp is tucked off the trail and appears abandoned suddenly rather than looted.

Established clues:

- a tent still stands
- the firepit looks recently used
- weapons and supplies were left behind
- nothing appears packed up
- **Edrin's Knife**, marked with initials `E.V.`, can be discovered

Checks:

- DC 14 Investigation — find Edrin's Knife
- DC 11 Survival — fresh human footprints leave camp heading north
- DC 13 Survival — footprints become erratic as though Edrin became confused or panicked
- DC 15 Survival — notice drag marks pulling toward the Hollow

### Hollow approach

Visual/environmental language established for the approach:

- living forest gradually turns grey
- dead leaves increase
- plants stop appearing alive
- the beaten path continues toward the dead zone
- footprints can be discovered on the path

### Hazard room

A combat/hazard room uses four circular marked areas.

At the end of each round, creatures remaining in marked circles take damage. The purpose is to force movement while combat continues.

## First Hollow resolution

Jess's father and Zach's Dog are intended to be found in the first Hollow sequence, along with captured villagers/prisoners.

A jail-cell area is part of the intended visual/story setup for these prisoners.

The first Hollow culminates in the destruction/defeat of its **Catalyst**, after which the party needs a reason to continue to another Hollow rather than simply going home.

## Next major location — Veyrholt

The next town/location after Greymere is **Veyrholt**.

Veyrholt should share the same broader artistic/world aesthetic as Greymere while being distinct enough to feel like a new chapter.

The party should travel through multiple Hollows over the campaign, leveling up and learning more about Hollow history and behavior before the campaign's largest truth is revealed.

Veyrholt is now locked across `docs/campaign/chapters/VEYRHOLT.md` plus the newer mechanic override in `docs/campaign/chapters/VEYRHOLT_BELL_REVISION.md`.

Lasting canon introduced there:

- Castle Veyr stands inside a long-established Hollow that Veyrholt's people learned to farm, travel, and build around.
- Its renewed activity begins on the same night the Greymere Catalyst is destroyed. This remains a correlation, not confirmed causation.
- Veyrholt's signature phenomenon is **the Answer**: ringing a specially tuned bell creates a Call, and about six seconds later the Hollow answers through the most recently rung tuned bell. The Answer does **not** replay attacks, spells, movement, or other actions.
- The old builders learned to exploit that six-second rule to ground and redirect Hollow resonance, creating the bell network around the town and Castle Veyr.
- The apparently mundane livestock disappearances are an ancient tithe process using inherited tuned bells to call the animals back toward the castle.
- The party learns the bell rule progressively and ultimately uses it against the Bell Regent by redirecting its own Answers into the anchor bells sustaining it.
- Ser Caldris Veyr became the **Bell Regent**, a constructed Catalyst used to impose a predictable civic pattern on something older.
- Catalysts can therefore be built or installed as restraints. This does not explain the origin of all Catalysts or the campaign's final world-scale truth.
- The chapter ends with a route to **Lornwatch Abbey**, where the expected watcher is absent.
- The meaning of `VII`, the relationship between the two Hollow events, and the inconsistent doorway sketches remain unresolved.

Veyrholt NPCs should use clear modern conversational language when delivering important lore or mechanic explanations. Old-fashioned wording is appropriate for ancient inscriptions, preserved oaths, and deliberate atmosphere, not for ordinary exposition the players need to understand.

## Canon discipline

When a detail is marked as "considered," "possible," "planned," or otherwise uncertain, do not silently promote it to immutable canon.

When creating new lore:

- connect it to at least one established thread where useful
- avoid explaining every mystery immediately
- record major lasting additions back into the campaign docs
- distinguish player-known facts from DM-only truth
